## What is WordPress? (WordPress.org vs WordPress.com explained)

WordPress is a content management system (CMS) that lets you create, manage, and publish website content without coding. Think of it as powerful software that provides an intuitive interface where you can write content, upload images, and design your site visually — no HTML, CSS, or JavaScript knowledge required.

> Here's where beginners get confused — there are two versions of WordPress, and they're very different.

**WordPress.org** is the self-hosted version. This is what most people mean when they say "WordPress." You download the free WordPress software and install it on your own web hosting account. This gives you complete control, full customization freedom, any themes or plugins you want, total monetization control, and ownership of your site and data. This is what we’ll focus on in this guide.

**WordPress.com** is a hosted service that handles all technical aspects for you. WordPress.com hosts your site and manages security, backups, and updates automatically. However, it comes with significant limitations: restricted customization, limited plugin installation, WordPress.com branding on free plans, monetization restrictions, and less SEO control.

**Which should you choose?** WordPress.org is right if you're building a business website, want full customization, plan to monetize, need professional capabilities, want to build an ecommerce store, or are willing to invest minimal time learning for unlimited possibilities. WordPress.com makes sense only for simple personal blogs or hobby sites where customization isn’t such a big deal.

For entrepreneurs, small business owners, and anyone building a site with professional goals, WordPress.org is the better choice. Modern hosting providers like GoDaddy have streamlined the process with one-click installation, making self-hosting nearly as simple as WordPress.com but without restrictions.

## Step 1: Choose your domain name and hosting

Every website needs two elements — a domain name (your website's address) and web hosting (where your website lives). Understanding [what a domain name](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/what-is-a-domain-name) is and [what](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/what-is-web-hosting) hosting is helps you see how these work together.

When someone types your domain into their browser, it points to your hosting server, which delivers your website. While you can purchase a domain and hosting separately, getting both from the same provider simplifies management.

Choosing quality hosting from the start is a big deal. Cheap hosting costs you through slow loading times, frequent downtime, security vulnerabilities, poor support, and difficulty scaling. Think of hosting as your house's foundation. Cutting corners affects everything built on top.

### Pick the perfect domain name

Your domain name is a big part of your brand identity. Follow these principles:

- Keep it short and memorable (2-3 words typically work best).
- Use .com when possible. It's the most recognized and trusted.
- Avoid numbers and hyphens that create confusion when sharing verbally.
- Make it brandable rather than generic.
- Consider SEO keywords if they don't compromise memorability.
- Check social media availability for consistent branding.

With those ideas in mind, let’s run through some domain dos and don'ts:

| **Do** | **Don’t** |
| --- | --- |
| Make it easy to spell and pronounce | Use too many numbers or special characters |
| Keep it unique | Choose something too similar to competitors |
| Check for trademarks | Use trademarked terms |
| Consider how it sounds out loud | Make it so creative people don't understand what you do |

[Check domain availability](https://www.godaddy.com/domains) or use the [domain name generator](https://www.godaddy.com/domains/domain-name-generator) for suggestions if your first choice is taken. For more guidance, see these [10 tips for choosing the perfect domain name](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/10-tips-for-choosing-the-perfect-domain-name).

At this point, you should also consider adding [domain privacy and protection](https://www.godaddy.com/domains/domain-protection) to mask your personal information from the public WHOIS database, protecting you from spam and identity theft.

### Choose the right type of hosting

Different hosting types serve different needs:

- **Shared hosting**: Most affordable, but shares resources with many sites
- **VPS hosting**: Provides dedicated resources but requires technical knowledge
- **Dedicated hosting**: Offers maximum performance but is expensive and complex
- **Managed WordPress hosting**: Specifically optimized for WordPress, handling technical aspects automatically (ideal for beginners and busy professionals)

Managed WordPress hosting combines affordability with WordPress-specific optimization, automatic updates, enhanced security, expert support, and features like caching and CDN integration.

## Step 3: Essential WordPress settings and configuration

Before customizing the appearance of your budding website, spend a few minutes on the settings affecting everything you build. These are mostly one-time configurations that prevent problems later and optimize your site for search engines and security.

For deeper insights, explore these [top WordPress tricks](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/top-wordpress-tricks) to enhance your experience.

### Getting to know the WordPress dashboard

Your WordPress dashboard at _yourdomain.com/wp-admin_ is mission control for your entire website. When you first log in, you'll see a clean, organized interface with the main navigation menu running down the left side of your screen.

This is where you'll spend most of your time managing and building your site. Key sections include:

- **Posts** for creating and managing blog content, your dated articles that appear in chronological order
- **Media** for your image library, storing all photos, videos, and documents you upload
- **Pages** for static website pages like your homepage, About, and Contact pages
- **Comments** for moderation, where you can approve, reply to, or delete visitor comments
- **Appearance** for design customization, including themes, menus, and widgets
- **Plugins** for adding functionality and extending what WordPress can do
- **Users** for managing who has access to your site and their permission levels
- **Settings** for fundamental configurations that control how your entire site operates

The dashboard also displays a helpful overview section showing recent activity, quick drafts, and site statistics. At the top of your screen, you'll find a toolbar with shortcuts to visit your live site, check for updates, and access your user profile.

Take time to click around and explore each section — don’t worry, you can't break anything by looking — and familiarizing yourself with where everything is located will make the rest of your website-building journey much smoother and more intuitive.

### Configuring permalinks for SEO

Permalinks are permanent URLs for pages and posts. By default, WordPress creates ugly URLs like yourdomain.com/?p=123.

> For better SEO and user experience, change this.

Navigate to **Settings > Permalinks**. Select **Post name**. Click **Save Changes**. This creates clean URLs like yourdomain.com/about-us that include keywords and are human-readable. Do this early before sharing links to avoid broken URLs later.

### Critical security settings

Bad actors, beware! You can implement basic security precautions without becoming a cybersecurity expert. Use a strong administrator username and password (change at **Users > Your Profile**).

Enable two-factor authentication following this guide on [enabling 2FA for WordPress](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/wordpress-security-guide#two-factor-authentication). Keep WordPress, themes, and plugins updated regularly. Limit login attempts via security plugin. Be cautious with administrator access and use appropriate user roles.

### Privacy and comment settings

Navigate to **Settings > Discussion** to configure comments. Check "Allow people to submit comments on new posts." Require name and email. Enable "Comment must be manually approved" to prevent spam from appearing publicly. Check "Comment author must have a previously approved comment" so regular commenters aren't held up after first approval.

For spam management, see [managing spam in WordPress](https://www.godaddy.com/help/manage-my-wordpress-comment-settings-26360).

### Set your WordPress website to coming soon

If you're not ready to launch, enable coming soon mode to display a placeholder while you work. Many themes include built-in coming soon options. Alternatively, install a plugin like SeedProd or Coming Soon Page. Navigate to **Plugins > Add New**, search, install, activate, and configure your coming soon page. Remember to disable it when launching!

With settings configured, your WordPress foundation is solid. Now let's make your site look like your brand.

## Step 4: Choose and customize your WordPress theme

There’s no overstating the importance of how your website looks, so let’s take a sec to discuss [how to use WordPress themes](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/making-sense-of-wordpress-templates). A theme incorporates elements like the visual design, layout, colors, and typography. You can also use [GoDaddy Airo for WordPress](https://www.godaddy.com/hosting/wordpress-hosting) to fine-tune your theme, producing a beautiful design that’s ready to go right out of the box.

### Free vs premium WordPress themes

**Free themes** are available through the WordPress directory ( **Appearance > Themes > Add New**). They're perfect for getting started, testing WordPress, or building simple sites with limited budgets. Advantages include zero cost, easy installation, and being good enough for a simple website. Limitations include fewer customization options, potentially no dedicated support, fewer advanced features, and less uniqueness.

**Premium themes** ($30-$60 typically) offer extensive customization, professional support, regular updates, advanced features like page builders, multiple demo layouts, and optimized code for better performance.

For beginners, the best bet might be to start with a quality free theme, and then upgrade to premium if you outgrow it. For business sites where first impressions matter, investing in premium from the start saves time.

### How to evaluate theme quality

Once you start shopping themes, it’s easy to get overwhelmed by all the cool designs. But it’s important to focus. This is an important choice.

Evaluate themes against these criteria:

- **Last update date**: Within 6 months indicates active maintenance
- **Active installations and ratings**: Thousands of installations and 4.5+ stars show reliability
- **Mobile responsive design**: This one’s non-negotiable because 60%+ of traffic is mobile
- **Page speed**: Lightweight themes load faster
- **Browser compatibility:** Ensures a better experience for visitors
- **Documentation quality**: Comprehensive docs save hours
- **Customization options**: Abundant but not overwhelming

Red flags include no updates in over a year, poor ratings, very few installations, no mobile responsiveness mentioned, overwhelming negative reviews, no documentation, and themes trying to do everything.

For template selection tips, see [choosing the best website template](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/tips-for-picking-the-best-website-template).

### Installing and customizing your theme

**Free theme installation**: Navigate to **Appearance > Themes > Add New**. Search, click **Details & Preview**, then **Install** and **Activate**.

**Premium theme installation**: Download the .zip file, go to **Appearance > Themes > Add New > Upload Theme**, choose the file, **Install Now**, then **Activate**.

Customize through **Appearance > Customize**. Common options include site identity (logo, title), colors, fonts, header/footer settings, homepage settings, menus, and widgets. Changes preview live before publishing.

For detailed instructions, see [how to install a WordPress theme](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/how-to-install-a-wordpress-theme-a-beginners-guide).

### Mobile responsiveness check

Test mobile responsiveness using the Customizer's preview modes, browser developer tools' responsive design mode, and your actual smartphone. Ensure text is readable without zooming, images display properly, navigation works, buttons are tappable, forms are usable, and layout looks intentional.

If your theme isn't mobile-friendly, switch themes rather than trying to fix it. See [how to make WordPress sites mobile-friendly](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/make-wordpress-sites-mobile-friendly).

## Common WordPress problems (and how to troubleshoot them)

![A Cursor Hovers over the WordPress Settings Menu Icon](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/image-1.jpeg?size=3840x0)Source: [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/photos/settings-options-software-website-265131/)

Most common WordPress issues have straightforward solutions that even beginners can fix with a step-by-step approach to troubleshooting. The key is not to panic when something goes wrong. Take a deep breath and work through the problem systematically.

> For comprehensive troubleshooting guidance, see [troubleshooting WordPress](https://www.godaddy.com/help/troubleshooting-wordpress-5675).

If you're really stuck or encounter a problem beyond your comfort level, don't hesitate to [contact GoDaddy support](https://www.godaddy.com/help/contact-us) if you're a customer, or consider hiring a WordPress professional who can quickly diagnose and resolve complex issues.

The key to troubleshooting is to always make one change at a time so you know exactly what fixed the problem. Keep recent backups before making significant changes, and document what you try so you can reverse course if needed.

Now let's run through some common errors and their solutions:

- **White screen of death**: Your site displays a blank white screen with no error message. This is typically caused by plugin conflicts or memory limits. Deactivate plugins by renaming your plugins folder via FTP from "plugins" to "plugins\_old." If your site works, reactivate plugins one by one to identify the culprit. [Fix white screen](https://www.godaddy.com/help/troubleshoot-and-fix-a-wordpress-white-screen-of-death-27220)
- **Database connection error**: WordPress can't connect to your database. Verify database credentials (name, username, password, host) in your wp-config.php file match what's in your hosting control panel. If they’re correct but the error is still there, contact your host as the database server may be down. [Fix database error](https://www.godaddy.com/help/fix-error-establishing-a-database-connection-in-wordpress-26356)
- **500 internal error**: A generic server error without specifics. Often caused by corrupted .htaccess files. Fix it by going to Settings > Permalinks and clicking Save Changes without changing anything. [Fix internal error](https://www.godaddy.com/help/fix-a-wordpress-internal-server-error-40406)
- **404 errors**: Pages exist but show "Page Not Found." Your permalink structure needs resetting. Go to Settings > Permalinks and click Save Changes to regenerate the .htaccess file. [Fix 404 error](https://www.godaddy.com/help/reset-wordpress-permalinks-to-fix-the-404-error-26351)
- **Plugin conflicts**: New plugin causes site problems or errors. Deactivate all plugins to confirm that plugins are the issue. If the site works, reactivate plugins one at a time, checking your site after each activation. When the problem returns, you've found the problematic plugin. [Fix plugin errors](https://www.godaddy.com/help/fix-wordpress-plugin-errors-26341)
- **Slow loading**: Site becomes sluggish. Install a caching plugin, compress images with optimization plugins, delete unused plugins and themes, optimize your database, or upgrade hosting if necessary. Run speed tests to identify specific bottlenecks. [Troubleshoot slow sites](https://www.godaddy.com/help/troubleshooting-a-slow-wordpress-site-19533)
- **Stuck in maintenance mode**: After updates, your site displays "Briefly unavailable for scheduled maintenance" permanently. This happens when the .maintenance file isn't deleted. Connect via FTP and delete the .maintenance file from your WordPress root directory. [Fix maintenance mode](https://www.godaddy.com/help/fix-my-wordpress-site-stuck-in-maintenance-mode-40898)

## FAQs about building a WordPress website

### How much does a WordPress website cost?

The WordPress core software is free, but there can be some additional costs, such as hosting ($10-30/month) and domain registration ($10+/year) — roughly $130-$380 for the first year. Optional costs include a premium theme ($30-60), premium plugins ($20-100+/year), and professional email ($5-10/month). Most beginners build professional sites for $150-$300 for the first year.

### Can I build a WordPress site for free?

Technically yes, using WordPress.com's free plan, but with severe limitations:

- Forced branding
- No custom domain
- Restricted customization
- No plugins
- Limited monetization

It’s fair to say the free version is not suitable for professional sites, while self-hosted WordPress.org requires minimal investment in hosting and a domain.

### Do WordPress websites need a lot of maintenance?

Some maintenance is needed, but managed hosting handles much of it automatically. Each month, recommended tasks include:

- Update plugins/themes
- Monitor comments
- Publish new content
- Check functionality
- Review analytics

Overall, you should expect 30 to 60 minutes monthly for basic maintenance, plus content creation time.

### Do I need coding skills to create a WordPress site?

Absolutely not. WordPress was designed for non-technical users. Everything uses visual interfaces requiring no code. If you can use email, you can build a WordPress site. Optional coding knowledge (HTML/CSS/PHP) enables advanced customization — but isn't required.

### How long does it take to build a WordPress site?

For a basic functional site, expect four to eight hours of focused work. If your goal is a polished, content-rich site, you’re probably looking at several days to weeks, depending on content creation. Technical aspects take just hours, but content creation extends your timeline. The best strategy is to launch simply and improve over time — many successful sites start small and grow over time. Remember, scalability is one of the greatest strengths of WordPress.
